Abstract
This monograph details the research, development, and implementation of a low-complexity, privacy-preserving human presence tracking system utilizing an integrated 24 GHz millimetre-wave (mmWave) FMCW and Doppler radar module (RD-03D). Standard surveillance paradigms relying on computer vision architectures incur substantial processing overhead and compromise individual privacy. To bypass this, we propose a decoupled architecture that delegates primary electromagnetic signal processing to an on-chip Digital Signal Processor (DSP), returning clean target vectors ([distance, angle, velocity]) over a high-speed 256,000-baud UART interface to a Renesas RA4M1 32-bit MCU. We detail a three-point digital smoothing filter to reject multipath anomalies and map azimuth scans onto a custom real-time polar visualization. The system achieves complete environmental immunity under zero light conditions.

I. Electromagnetic Principles & Phase Shift

Classical passive infrared (PIR) sensors operate as simple differential thermal detectors, remaining fundamentally blind to stationary human targets due to their reliance on high-frequency thermal transitions across discrete detection quadrants. To resolve this sensing deficit without introducing high-bandwidth video surveillance channels, we employ millimetre-wave radar operating within the 24.00 GHz to 24.25 GHz industrial, scientific, and medical (ISM) band[1].

The fundamental physics of our presence sensing engine rests on the Doppler Effect and frequency-modulated continuous wave (FMCW) radar equations. When an electromagnetic wave of frequency $f_c$ is radiated towards a target, the reflected wave's frequency shift $f_d$ correlates directly to the target's relative radial velocity $v$:

f_d = \frac{2 \cdot v \cdot f_c}{c} \cdot \cos(\theta)

Where $c$ represents the speed of light, and $\theta$ is the angle of approach relative to the transceiver's major coordinate axis. For static human presence detection, the engine targets micro-Doppler signatures. The chest wall displacement during quiet respiration exhibits an amplitude range of $0.3\text{ mm}$ to $1.0\text{ mm}$ at a frequency range of $0.15\text{ Hz}$ to $0.4\text{ Hz}$. Operating at $24\text{ GHz}$, the wavelength ($\lambda$) is approximately $12.5\text{ mm}$. A $0.5\text{ mm}$ chest displacement yields a noticeable phase shift $\Delta\Phi$:

\Delta\Phi = \frac{4\pi \cdot \Delta d}{\lambda} \approx 0.50 \text{ radians}

This phase variation is detected by the RD-03D's dual-channel quadrature receiver and parsed internally, outputting verified target parameters without demanding raw Fourier transforms (FFT) from the primary microcontroller.

II. Real-Time Multi-Layer Data Pipeline

To maintain highly responsive, low-latency target tracking under low processing budgets, the hardware leverages a strict 4-layer decoupled signal processing pipeline. By abstracting raw electromagnetic calculations into discrete stages, we bypass memory and scheduling congestion:

Layer Responsibility Hardware/Software Processing Bound
L1: Sensing Continuous FMCW sweep, signal collection, Phase extraction On-chip RD-03D DSP Hardware Bound (Real-time)
L2: Communication de-packetization, checksum validation, 256,000-baud frame streaming High-speed UART interrupt ~0.1ms per frame
L3: Processing Moving-average smoothing, outlier rejection, formatting to CSV Arduino Uno R4 (RA4M1) ~2.0ms per frame
L4: Visualization Polar coordinate rendering, target drawing, trajectory trail logging Processing IDE (Host PC) 30 FPS (Fluid)

A critical challenge in indoor radar processing is multipath clutter — reflections from walls, metallic server enclosures, and door frames which produce static "ghost" targets. To mitigate this, Layer 3 applies a rolling three-point moving average filter. Let $x[n]$ represent the raw incoming parameter frame at index $n$. The filtered output $y[n]$ is formulated as:

y[n] = \frac{x[n] + x[n-1] + x[n-2]}{3}

This sliding digital filter suppresses high-frequency noise and sudden signal loss, producing smooth tracking coordinates without inducing significant phase delay.

IV. Rapid Prototyping & Development Timeline

The construction, integration, and code refinement for this radar package was completed across a highly focused 6-day rapid engineering cycle. This approach prioritized early hardware validation followed by aggressive DSP iteration:

Day 01 - Specification
Protocol Mapping
Analyzed the RD-03D UART command architecture and payload formatting. Documented target tracking register structures.
Day 02 - Assembly
HIL Wiring
Assembled circuit boards. Added dual-rail decoupling capacitors on 5V supply line to eliminate servo induction hazards.
Day 03 - Firmware
Interrupt Parsing
Developed the circular ring-buffer UART parser for the Renesas RA4M1 32-bit controller, achieving zero frame drops.
Day 04 - Visualizer
Polar Projection
Constructed the host-PC visualization scope in Processing, transforming raw angular coordinates to cartesian space.
Day 05 - Verification
Clutter Profiling
Mapped classroom and lab physical spaces. Documented multipath signals from wall and window reflections.
Day 06 - Calibration
Firmware Tweaking
Optimized clutter thresholds and finalized moving average coefficient parameters inside the microcontroller source.
