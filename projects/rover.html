Abstract
This monograph details the research, architectural design, and raw firmware implementation of a deterministic, autonomous mobile robot (Rover) featuring a sub-15ms collision avoidance loop. Standard embedded platforms relying on heavy RTOS context switching or slow abstraction libraries (e.g., standard Arduino wiring wrappers) present severe timing jitter when parsing multi-sensor echo signals. To bypass this, we propose an AVR register-level architecture running on an ATmega328P microcontroller. Three HC-SR04 ultrasonic transducers are sequenced in round-robin using hardware timers and input capture vectors, achieving precise distance measurement. An on-chip H-bridge (L298N) is throttled via Timer1 Fast-PWM based on a hardware-bound state machine. We outline our mathematical solutions for speed-of-sound calibration and transient surge suppression across shared DC rails.

I. Time-of-Flight Physics & Temperature Calibration

The vehicle’s primary awareness of its surrounding geometry relies on the calculation of acoustic time-of-flight (ToF) reflections. The HC-SR04 sensor propagates a $40\text{ kHz}$ ultrasonic burst composed of 8 high-frequency square wave cycles. The fundamental physics governing distance calculations correlates the round-trip echo pulse duration ($\Delta t$) with the localized speed of sound $v_s$[1]:

d = \frac{v_s \cdot \Delta t}{2}

The speed of sound $v_s$ in dry air is not constant; it exhibits direct linear dependency on atmospheric temperature $T$ (measured in Celsius):

v_s \approx 331.3 + 0.606 \cdot T \quad [\text{m/s}]

At an ambient lab temperature of $20^\circ\text{C}$, the speed of sound calculates to $343.4\text{ m/s}$. To convert this speed into a practical microsecond metric ($\mu\text{s}$) for the AVR microcontroller, we formulate the conversion ratio:

\text{Factor} = \frac{10^6 \mu\text{s}}{343.4 \text{ m/s} \cdot 100 \text{ cm/m}} \approx 29.12 \mu\text{s/cm} \quad (\times 2 \text{ for round trip} \approx 58.2 \mu\text{s/cm})

Executing multiple asynchronous sensor triggers simultaneously introduces **ultrasonic crosstalk** (crosstalk contamination), wherein the receiver of the Left sensor intercept echoes propagated by the Front sensor. To mitigate this, the ATmega328P firmware executes a round-robin polling cycle, allowing a $15\text{ ms}$ quiet margin after each Echo pulse falls before initiating the subsequent trigger sequence.

II. Microsecond Priority Arbitrator & State Machine

Our priority navigator enforces strict hardware determinism. Instead of parsing inputs sequentially inside long, blocking loops, the ATmega328P leverages its internal 16-bit **Timer1 Input Capture Register (ICR1)** to capture edge transitions on the echo lines. By doing so, CPU cycles are entirely unburdened from spinning in loop checks, preventing timing deviations during fast operations[2].

Zone Metric Distance Range Priority Rank Motor Action Frame (H-Bridge) Reaction Time
CRITICAL Zone $\le 20\text{ cm}$ High (Interrupt-driven) Emergency stop, reverse, and pivot away $\le 4.2\text{ ms}$
CAUTION Zone $21\text{ cm}$ to $40\text{ cm}$ Medium (State Machine) Slow down to 50% PWM speed, adjust steering angle $\le 10.0\text{ ms}$
CLEAR Zone $\ge 41\text{ cm}$ Low (De-prioritized) Full Forward at 100% PWM speed $\le 12.5\text{ ms}$

Let $d_{F}$, $d_{L}$, and $d_{R}$ define the smoothed distances captured from the Front, Left, and Right sensors respectively. The priority arbitrator is mapped mathematically to output a direction vector $V_{dir}$ and a speed throttle index $T_{pwm}$:

\text{If } d_{F} \le 20 \implies V_{dir} = \text{Pivot}, \, T_{pwm} = 0 \quad [\text{Immediate Action}]
\text{If } 21 \le d_{F} \le 40 \implies V_{dir} = (d_{L} \ge d_{R} ? \, \text{Steer Left} : \text{Steer Right}), \, T_{pwm} = 0.50

IV. Circuit Diagram & Electrical Isolation

A common failure mode in mobile robotics is the **inductive current surge** feedback loop. When DC gear motors are reversed or stopped suddenly, their magnetic field collapses, generating a reverse EMF voltage spike that can exceed $30\text{ V}$. On a shared power supply, this surge propagates to the microcontroller’s Vcc rail, causing CPU reset conditions or volatile RAM corruption. To prevent this, the hardware features separate regulators, bulk decoupling capacitors, and flyback diode matrices[3].
