Abstract
This monograph details the research, digital signal processing (DSP) architecture, and firmware optimization of an infrastructure-free, full-duplex voice transceiver (Walkie-Talkie) built on the dual-core 32-bit Xtensa LX6 architecture (ESP32). Wireless audio links on embedded nodes are severely limited by RF channel congestion and environmental acoustic clutter. We propose a decoupled, double-stage dsp pipe operating per 20ms frame: Spectral Subtraction mathematically models and subtracts the stationary noise floor, while a 32-tap Normalized Least Mean Squares (NLMS) filter converges asynchronously to suppress narrowband interference and acoustic echo. Transmission over connectionless ESP-NOW air frames bypasses router handshakes, achieving a sub-15ms E2E pipeline delay with ~12dB SNR gain.

I. Noise Cancellation Mathematics & NLMS Convergence

In typical environments, speech intelligibility is heavily degraded by both stationary broadband noise (fan hum, white noise) and non-stationary narrowband tones (50Hz mains power line interference). To isolate speech without relying on deep-neural networks beyond embedded limits, we deploy a two-stage DSP pipeline.

The first stage, **Spectral Subtraction**, estimates the noise power spectral density $|N(f)|^2$ during non-speech periods. During active speech, this model is subtracted directly from the input spectrum $|X(f)|^2$:

|\hat{S}(f)|^2 = \max\left(|X(f)|^2 - \beta \cdot |\hat{N}(f)|^2, \ \alpha \cdot |X(f)|^2\right)

Where $\beta$ is the over-subtraction factor and $\alpha$ is the spectral floor parameter to suppress musical noise artifacts.

The second stage is a **Normalized Least Mean Squares (NLMS)** adaptive filter. The standard LMS filter updates weights $w(n)$ in the direction of the negative gradient of the mean square error. However, its convergence speed is highly dependent on input signal power. NLMS solves this by normalizing the step-size parameter $\mu$ by the squared Euclidean norm of the input vector $x(n)$:

w(n+1) = w(n) + \frac{\mu}{\|x(n)\|^2 + \epsilon} \cdot e(n) \cdot x(n)

Where $e(n)$ is the error signal and $\epsilon$ is a small regularization constant to prevent division by zero in complete silence. This formulation ensures fast convergence (under 50 frames) and absolute stability across volatile vocal amplitudes.

II. Real-Time Audio Signal Processing Chain

The entire audio processing pipeline on the ESP32 operates deterministically in a fixed 20ms block-wise configuration, balancing real-time execution against DSP buffer depth:

01
I2S Capture INGEST
INMP441 digital MEMS microphone captures 8kHz / 16-bit PCM audio. Direct I2S DMA transfers samples in the background to avoid CPU polling. Interrupt fires every 20ms block (160 samples).
f_s = 8000Hz · bit_depth = 16 · block = 160 samples
~0.1ms
02
Hann-Windowed FFT FREQUENCY DOMAIN
Applies a 256-point Hann window to the frame to prevent spectral leakage, followed by an on-chip Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) to convert time-domain samples to spectral bins.
X(f) = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x(n) \cdot w(n) \cdot e^{-j 2\pi f n / N}
~2.0ms
03
Spectral Subtraction DENOISE
Subtracts the recursively estimated stationary noise PSD from the speech magnitude spectrum. Half-wave rectification clamps negative values to prevent phase artifacts.
Ŝ(f) = |Ŝ(f)| · e^{j \angle X(f)}
~1.0ms
04
NLMS Adaptive Filter ADAPTIVE
Converts back to time-domain (IFFT). A 32-tap NLMS filter processes the temporal stream to suppress residual narrow tone humming and acoustic echoes.
e(n) = y(n) - w^T(n) \cdot x(n)
~1.5ms
05
ESP-NOW Low-Latency TX WIRELESS
Compresses 16-bit PCM to 8-bit µ-law to reduce bandwidth. Transmits 160-byte payload over connectionless ESP-NOW. Air latency is negligible (<250µs) due to lack of TCP handshakes.
µ-law compression ratio = 2:1 · air_time ≈ 250µs
~4.0ms

IV. Wireless Protocol Latency Benchmarks

Traditional Wi-Fi architectures require continuous association handshakes ($DHCP \rightarrow TCP \ ACK$). When nodes experience physical dropouts, TCP retransmissions block the queue, causing latency spikes beyond $300\text{ms}$.

Our Walkie-Talkie bypasses this constraint by implementing **ESP-NOW**. It communicates directly at the 802.11 physical layer, eliminating routing, IP assignments, and cloud handshakes:

Protocol Standard Underlying Layer Router Requirement Throughput Capability Typical Latency Bounds
ESP-NOW (Local Peer Link) Raw 802.11 PHY Frame None (Direct RF) 250 kbps < 5.0 ms (Stable)
Wi-Fi TCP Socket TCP / IP Stack Required (Central AP) Up to 150 Mbps 120 ms — 350 ms
Bluetooth Classic (SPP) RFCOMM / L2CAP None (P2P Pair) Up to 2.1 Mbps 30 ms — 80 ms
